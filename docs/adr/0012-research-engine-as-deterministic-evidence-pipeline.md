# Research engine as a deterministic evidence pipeline without main search

The research engine is a deterministic four-stage evidence pipeline — plan, discover, fetch, account-and-deliver — that performs neither semantic synthesis nor retrieval orientation. Main search (grok web/x-search) deliberately stays out of the engine: orientation belongs to the skill layer, where the agent reads a `forager search` result *before* writing the plan, so the retrieval landscape can shape decomposition and high-value citation URLs enter subquestion text as known-URL evidence directives. An in-engine parallel orientation stage was rejected because the plan is already fixed when it runs — it cannot prevent a wrong split — and it would admit LLM-synthesized content into a pipeline whose evidence contract is fetch-before-claim.

## Consequences

- Budget tiers constrain shape instead of one global counter: subquestion caps 2/4/6 (quick/standard/deep) and per-subquestion evidence caps 1/2/3. Bare `research` defaults to standard; callers choose deep explicitly when its maximum coverage and provider cost are warranted. The caps are upper bounds, not fill targets — a subquestion with at least one accepted evidence item is covered. Discovery limit = cap × 3, applied as the per-capability request limit and equally as the merged, post-dedup candidate pool bound per subquestion. The global required-evidence floor (1 or 2, intent-driven) is clamped to min(required, Σ caps) with the clamp disclosed, so no tier/plan combination is unsatisfiable by construction. Caller-injected plans over the subquestion cap exit 2; classifier plans are bounded up front (schema maxItems plus an importance-ordered prompt) and truncated with a diagnostic as fallback.
- Known URLs are first-class evidence directives outside the caps: URLs in subquestion text attach to that subquestion; query-only URLs are plan-scope and attach to no subquestion. All known URLs are always attempted (the plan author bears the cost of what the plan declares), each unique URL is fetched once, and a failed known-URL fetch always records an explicit gap and keeps the outcome degraded, even when substitute evidence lands.
- Gap accounting is subquestion-terminal: a failed candidate fetch records an attempt only; a gap means a subquestion exhausted its candidate pool without evidence, with the reason counting candidate URLs attempted rather than provider attempts. `closed`/`degraded` describe coverage of successful outcomes only; command-level errors keep the existing postflight attribution order (Timeout, Runtime, Quality, Evidence, …) under the 600 s deadline rail. Capability gaps still degrade the outcome — an unexecutable declared capability is a real coverage hole, not a transport hiccup.
- DocsSearch read content (such as context7 full text) is accepted as evidence provider-neutrally via the seam contract — never by provider-id branching (F10) — and passes the same thin-content gate as fetched pages. It is not Normalized Fetch Content and does not re-enter WebFetch.
- The engine is single-pass. Refetch loops run in the agent layer, enabled by unconsumed-candidate disclosure and synthesis-policy declarations (fetch-before-claim; unsupported claims downgrade to unverified candidates). The mechanical synthesize report is removed; output field shapes belong to the output-contract ticket.
- Accepted limitation: bare CLI research (no agent in the loop) plans from query text alone — it has no pre-plan orientation and inherits weaker decomposition on ambiguous queries. Orientation requires the skill workflow (search, then write the plan).
